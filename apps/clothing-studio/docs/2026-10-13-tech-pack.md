# Automatic tech pack — issue #227

## Intended outcome and gap

A Fabra-style workflow ends with a manufacturer-ready tech pack generated from the design and kept in sync with it. After #226, Clothing Studio could style a tee in 3D but could only export renders and a GLB. Every factory document had to be written by hand and kept in step with the design manually. This is roadmap step 2 in the [Fabra research](2026-10-13-fabra-research.md).

## What was built

- **Spec on the product** (`src/product.ts`):
  - an optional `spec` holding the style number and season (at most 40 characters of plain text) and the sample size (`XS`–`XL`), validated and undoable;
  - `teeDimensions()` is now the single source for the tee's derived dimensions, used by the pattern pieces, the 3D silhouette and the tech pack.
- **Builder** (`src/techpack.ts`, pure). `buildTechPack(product, date)` returns:
  - **Bill of materials.** Pattern pieces are grouped by identical part style, and consumption is computed as Σ area × cut count × 1.15. Trims and artwork lines are added.
  - **POM A–K.** Values come from `teeDimensions`, rounded to 0.5 cm, with tolerances. Grading uses fixed per-size increments (for example chest ½ ±2.5 cm, length ±1.5 cm, long sleeves ±1 cm), centred on the chosen sample size, so the sample column always equals the design.
  - **Construction rows**, which depend on the neckline and artwork.
  - Notes that state the estimates' limits.
- **Exports**:
  - `techPackXlsx` writes Office Open XML directly: a store-only ZIP with CRC-32, inline strings, numeric measurement cells, a bold header style and a Normal cell style. It has no dependency.
  - `techPackHtml` produces a self-contained document with a restrictive CSP (`default-src 'none'; img-src data:`). It escapes all text and embeds renders only when they are `data:image/png;base64` URLs.
  - `measurementDiagram` draws the flat front and sleeve with labelled arrows.
- **Editor**: a tech pack section with detail fields, a live preview (BoM, diagram, POM, construction) re-rendered with the pattern pieces, and the two downloads. The printable pack captures front and back renders from the 3D view when WebGL is available.

## Verification (run on 2026-10-13)

- `npm run check` in `apps/clothing-studio` passed: 77 unit tests, lint and the production build.
- `tests/techpack.test.ts` covers:
  - fabric grouping for the ringer template;
  - consumption range and its growth with chest;
  - POM codes, sample column equality and monotonic grading;
  - re-centring on sample size L;
  - V-neck drop and mitre;
  - artwork placement text;
  - a defaulted spec;
  - the workbook, parsed by an in-test ZIP reader that checks every CRC: sheet names, escaping, inline strings and numeric cells;
  - escaping in the printable HTML, rejection of a `javascript:` render, and the diagram labels.
- `tests/techpack.spec.ts` (Chromium):
  - the preview follows the template;
  - details are saved and re-centre the grade;
  - a chest change updates POM A to 62;
  - the Excel download is a ZIP;
  - the printable download contains two PNG renders, opens on its own, and its images decode;
  - details survive a reload.
- Full browser suite: 80 passed.
- An independent reader, openpyxl 3.1.5 (run with warnings as errors), opened the downloaded workbook. It found the four sheets, bold headers and numeric size columns, for example chest XS–XL `54.5 … 64.5` with L as the sample.
- Screenshots of the printable pack and the V-neck diagram were reviewed. Overlapping E/G labels were moved.

## Limits

- Consumption is an area estimate, not a marker. Grading increments are generic tee values, not a brand's size chart.
- The tech pack covers the tee base only, until more base products arrive (roadmap step 4).
- Costing and colorways are roadmap step 3.
- The workbook was not opened in Microsoft Excel or LibreOffice here. Neither is installed. openpyxl and the in-test reader are the evidence.
