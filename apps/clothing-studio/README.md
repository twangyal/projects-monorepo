# Clothing Studio

A browser garment-design workspace. It is for designers who want to go from a template to a styled 3D garment, linked pattern pieces, a tech pack and a costed range. You pick a base product, style each part in real-time 3D, adjust measurements and construction, drape garments on an approximate body, and export renders, 3D models, patterns and spreadsheets. It runs entirely in the browser: no account, backend or paid service.

**Status:** ACTIVE — portfolio priority. Direction: [docs/PRODUCT_DIRECTION.md](../../docs/PRODUCT_DIRECTION.md)

## What works today

- **3D-first workspace.** The editable garment is the main screen. Focused tool sections cover Templates, Materials & prints, Artwork, Patterns & measurements, Body & drape, and Exports. Secondary dialogs open the tech pack, range plan, concept sketch and skirt construction. It works on desktop and phone.
- **Base products and templates**: T-shirt (Blank tee, Breton stripe, Ringer tee, Garden print), Polo shirt, Tank top, Crop top, T-shirt dress, Crew sweatshirt, Sweatshirt dress, Pullover hoodie, Zip hoodie, Pull-on shorts, Straight trousers, Joggers, Flared skirt and Gathered skirt. A top 52 cm long or less (down to 40 cm) ends above the waist and is named a crop top (or a cropped sweatshirt or hoodie); one long enough to pass the hips (85 cm or more, up to 120 cm) is a dress: it drapes over the hips and thighs and is named as a dress in the tech pack and patterns. Modules switch hem shape, sleeve length and a chest pocket. **Polo collar and placket** turns a round-necked T-shirt into a polo: a flat-knit collar replaces the neck rib, cut as a rectangle as long as the neckline between the placket's edges, and a three-button placket runs down the centre front. In 3D the collar stands about 2 cm and folds down, its points lying on the chest either side of the placket; the tech pack gives the collar height, placket length and how both are sewn, and "polo" in a description does the same. A T-shirt with a sleeve length of 0 (the Sleeveless preset) is a tank top: no sleeve pieces, its armholes bound and left open, and named as a tank top (or tank dress) in the tech pack. A skirt's waist can be gathered (1.1 to 2 times the finished waist, with the waist edge kept at least 10 cm shorter than the hem): the panels are cut with the longer waist edge, the drape draws it in so the extra fabric falls into gathers, and the tech pack says how far to gather it.
- **Part-by-part styling**: drag fabrics, colours and prints onto parts in 3D, or pick a part and click. The library has 8 fabrics with weight and composition, plus 6 print motifs. Repeat layout and size are in real centimetres. You can upload up to 6 of your own repeat motifs, and front/back artwork with size, position and rotation. **Print this text** sets words (up to 40 characters, wrapped at about 20 characters a line, in sans serif, serif or monospace and any colour) as a side's artwork. **Pick colours from a photo** (a product shot, mood picture or swatch) reads its main colours on the device, middle of the picture first, and keeps them as swatches with the design (not the photo); **Use them on the garment** puts the main colour on the body and a contrasting one on trims and prints in one undoable step. Artwork can be placed by clicking the 3D garment.
- **Describe a style or a garment**: a local, deterministic text parser (no AI). For one part it reads about 700 colour names (fashion names plus everyday names from the xkcd colour survey, with modifiers such as pale or dusty), fabrics, motifs, stripe direction, repeat size and layout. **Apply to the garment** also reads the garment type (starting from its template; "mini", "knee-length" and "midi" set a dress's length), neckline, sleeve length, hem, chest pocket, a hoodie's front zip ("zip hoodie", "zip-up", or "pullover" for none) and fit ("oversized", "cropped", "wide leg"), a gathered skirt waist, sleeveless and tank tops, crop tops ("cropped" ends a top above the waist), and styles each named part from its own clause ("oversized navy hoodie with a cream hood and white cuffs"). Words in quotes are printed on a top ("black tee with "Stay Wild" on the back in white letters"), in a colour that reads on the garment unless one is named. It reports each change with its scope and lists what it could not apply, including unsupported construction accents such as red topstitching, which do not change the garment colour. One Undo restores the design.
- **Linked measurements and fit**: each sleeve is drafted from its armhole, its cap cut 3% longer than the armhole it is sewn into. Body measurements, plus points of measure (shoulder, neck, armhole, hem, sleeve opening, shoulder drop, back neck, rib/cuff/hem-band heights, hood size, pocket placement, waistband height). These update the 3D garment, pattern pieces and tech pack together. Pattern handles can be dragged or moved with the keyboard. A hoodie's **Front zip** cuts the front, kangaroo pocket and hem band as mirrored halves, shows the zip down the centre front in 3D (the pocket halves stop short of it), and lists the open-end zip and how it is set in the tech pack. **Worn open** parts the front halves and hem band at the zip in 3D, each with its own side of the zip and the slider at the foot, and draped they hang apart (about 20 cm apart at the chest on the default body); the pattern pieces and tech pack stay the same. The hood stays a pullover hood, sewn to the neckline either side of the face opening, so the top of the parting opens wider than on a real zip hoodie.
- **Drape on a body** for every base: tees, sweatshirts, hoodies, flared skirts, shorts and trousers. Each garment is sewn from its pattern pieces: a top's front and back meet at the shoulder and side seams, its sleeves are set into the armholes as tubes along the arms, and rib cuffs and hem bands, cut 15% shorter, gather the edges they finish. An XPBD cloth solver runs in a Web Worker on a measurement-driven body proxy, shown as a smooth mannequin (its torso, arms, neck and head or legs blended into one dress-form surface, drawn just inside the shape the cloth keeps clear of) (chest, optional waist/hip girths and chest depth for tops; waist and hip for skirts, which hang from a held waistline, and for shorts and trousers, whose elastic waistband gathers onto the body waist above two legs). Each fabric's weight, bending rigidity and stretch stiffness change the result: on the same tee and body, knits cling while linen and denim stand off the body with smoother hems. You can compare two fabrics side by side on any garment, skirts and trousers included, and save top body profiles by name. An optional body height sets torso heights, arm length and trouser leg length, and the drape then says where the hem, sleeves and trouser legs end on that body ("the hems would reach 6 cm below the floor"). An optional waist-to-hip depth (5–40 cm) places the fullest hip below the waist on tops, bottoms and outfits, independently of girths and body height; blank keeps the existing proportions. A trouser proxy refuses a hip at or below its crotch. Applied chest, waist, hip, chest depth, height and hip-depth measurements stay with each design through reload, backups, the saved library and Undo/Redo; typing stays as an unapplied draft.
- **Outfits**: in Body & drape, **Wear with** pairs a top with shorts, trousers or a skirt (a template or a saved design), kept with the design as a copy and undoable. The bottoms drape first on the outfit's body, with their waistline at the body's waist, then the top drapes over them, untucked, and the 3D view shows both. **Tuck the top in** reverses the order for a top that is not a dress: the top drapes first, its body (not its sleeves) pressed close to the body below the waistband and then held there, and the bottoms drape over it, a skirt's waist easing out evenly to fit over it. Automatic waist and hip girths are ones both garments fit. The bottoms do not feel the top's weight. **Layers**: a T-shirt (a tee, tank, crop top or T-shirt dress) can also be worn under a sweatshirt or hoodie, the zip hoodie open or closed. Both are draped on a body posed for the outer top's arms, the T-shirt first; the outer top then rests on it as on a thicker body, keeping a little more than the T-shirt's own stand-off from the body (loose folds count for at most 2.5 cm, and under rib cuffs and bands the T-shirt is pressed flat), and wherever the outer top lies closer, the T-shirt is pressed in under it, so it shows only where it would (through an open zip, below a hem). Either garment can be the one edited. The T-shirt does not feel the outer top's weight, and a top worn over another is not tucked in. **And with** completes such an outfit with a third garment, so a T-shirt, a hoodie and joggers are worn together: the bottoms drape first, the T-shirt over them, then the outer top over both, outside the bottoms as well as the T-shirt. **Edit worn garment** goes round all three, and outfit exports include all three. Exports default to the design on its own; **Include worn garment in PNG and GLB** exports both garments in their settled positions, with their materials, prints and top artwork, without the mannequin. Drape the outfit first and wait for it to finish. The export keeps the captured outfit if you edit or remove a garment while it renders.
- **Sizes in 3D**: in Body & drape, **Size shown** displays and drapes any size from XS to XL, graded from the sample size, on the same body, so the fit of each size can be compared ("15 cm of ease at the chest" for an L where the M has 10). The design itself stays the sample size; 3D renders follow the size shown. If a later applied edit makes that grade unsupported, the view explicitly returns to the sample and explains the change without discarding the edit. Skirts use +4 cm of waist and +6 cm of hem per size, with side slant and finished band height fixed. These are starting grades to check with a fit sample.
- **Fabric drape test** beside each part's fabric: the garment solver drapes a 30 cm disc of that fabric over an 18 cm support, like a Cusick drape tester, and reports its drape coefficient, its number of folds and its shadow seen from above, next to the range drape testers report for that construction.
- **Material experiments**: tensile, bending and membrane swatch comparisons with SI-unit result downloads. These are illustrative and do not feed the garment solver.
- **Tech pack**: bill of materials with estimated consumption, graded points of measure (XS–XL) with a diagram, each size measured from the design graded to it (5 cm of chest and 1.5 cm of length per size for tops, 5 cm of waist and hip for shorts and trousers; fit points you edited keep their own increments), and construction steps. Exports as Excel (`.xlsx`) or a printable self-contained HTML file.
- **Colorways and range plan**: up to 8 named colorways with renders, plus costing inputs (fabric price per metre, make, trims, print, retail). The range plan shows unit cost, margin and totals, and exports to Excel.
- **Exports**: front/back PNG renders, a six-second turntable video (WebM, one turn around the styled or draped garment, outfit included if chosen), GLB 3D model (styled or draped), DXF pattern (AutoCAD R12, cut/sew/grain/fold/text layers, 1 cm seam allowance), and a 1:1 printable SVG pattern. **Pattern export size** chooses XS–XL using the same grades as the tech pack and 3D size preview, independently of the saved sample and the size shown in 3D. Downloads label that size; non-sample filenames include it. Sizes outside the supported measurements refuse with an explanation, and every skirt size uses its graded panels and band. Apply measurement drafts before exporting; the linked pattern previews stay at the sample size.
- **Product flared skirt**: style its front/back with the same fabric, print and colour tools as tops. Apply waist, hem circumference and side-seam slant together; linked annular-sector patterns, a rigid 3D shell, tech pack, colorways/costing and PNG/GLB/SVG/DXF exports follow. Body & drape simulates it held at the waist using the shared product solver and fabric library. **Skirt waistband height** adds an independently styled folded band above the waist seam (2–10 cm; 0 removes it), keeping the panel slant unchanged. Its cutting strip is twice the finished height, with a fold line in SVG/DXF, and its outer surface is sewn into the drape and included in 3D/renders and the tech pack. Old unbanded designs stay unchanged. The band’s inner facing, fold contact, interfacing and closure are not simulated; choose a closure and any extension separately. XS–XL size columns use the same relative grades as the size view and pattern exports; the legacy dialog’s saved grading rule stays separate.
- **Measured two-panel skirt** (legacy dialog, preserved independently): waist/hem/slant input, optional waistband, seam and hem allowances, and linear size grading. Exports include a full-size SVG, A4/US Letter tiled print HTML, and rigid or draped OBJ. Its drape simulation uses the product solver and the fabric library; skirts saved with the earlier presets (chiffon, cotton, linen, denim) drape as their library equivalents. **Replace 3D product with this skirt** copies the applied waist, hem, slant and any finished waistband height to a product skirt with default linen styling in one undoable edit. Applied legacy body waist, hip and waist-to-hip depth copy with the skirt; other product body settings stay. With no applied legacy body, all product body settings stay unchanged. The main drape uses a stadium-shaped proxy instead of circular rings, so the hang can differ and a body waist too large for the skirt can refuse. Legacy closure extension, allowances, fabric and grading remain separately saved. The copy is not linked to later legacy edits.
- **Concept sketchbook** (dialog): tee sketching, a photo overlay, and PNG/SVG/JSON export. Edits have Undo/Redo; consecutive title/note typing in one focused field is one step, preserving earlier garment edits. Leaving the field or making another edit starts a new step. Valid text still autosaves locally as you type, and a library holds up to 12 saved named copies. Autosave reuses checked images while you edit, avoiding repeated decoding of unchanged photos and graphics.

Trousers' **Ankle cuff height** makes them joggers: rib cuffs cut a third shorter than the leg opening gather it, the inseam is measured to the cuff edge (the leg panels stop the cuff's height short of it), and the cuffs are cut as two pattern pieces, listed in the tech pack with their points of measure and how they are set, and drawn in 3D, where draped legs gather into them at the ankle. "Joggers", "sweatpants" or "track pants" in a description do the same, and "shorts" takes the cuffs off.

Optional **underbust girth** gives the body a bust: the chest is then measured round the fullest part of the bust, the ribcage under it takes the underbust's girth, and two rounded forms in front of it are sized so that a tape round the bust measures the chest. The shoulders, arms and, without a height, the torso's lengths follow a frame between the two girths, and the automatic waist follows the ribcage. Tops and dresses drape over the bust and hang from it, in the mannequin, outfits, profiles, backups and fabric comparisons. Chest depth is left blank with a bust, which sets its own.

Optional **upper-arm girth** sets the upper arm on the proxy without narrowing it to fit the selected sleeve. It follows body profiles, project backups, outfits and matched fabric comparisons. Blank keeps automatic dimensions; forearms, wrists and shoulder placement stay assumed, and this does not validate fit.

Use **Edit worn garment** beside Wear with to switch between editing the two applied outfit garments. Both copies and shared body settings stay together; edits affect only the current garment. Switching is one Undo step, requires applied/discarded product drafts, and returns 3D and pattern export sizing to the new garment’s sample. The saved-library source stays separate.

## Known gaps

- Fabric properties (bending rigidity, membrane stiffness) are typical published values per fabric construction, not measurements of these fabrics. Bending is scaled per construction so each fabric's drape test lands mid-range in the coefficients reported for it (knits needed about three times their elastic rigidity, standing in for the yarn friction that holds real folds open); those ranges are approximate, and garments are not yet compared with reference drapes. Stretch depends on direction to the grain (wovens are soft on the bias, knits stretch more across), but the model is elastic: at garment-weight loads this barely changes the hang, and slow bias drop (creep) is not modelled. The 3D mesh is about 1.5-2 cm; a 1 cm mesh doubled the drape time without visibly changing the default tee, whose smooth hang comes from the T-posed proxy body rather than the mesh.
- Tops drape with the arms along the sleeves. Lowering the arms to a relaxed pose was tried: beyond about 50° below horizontal the underarm stretched by over 40%, because the solver pushes cloth to the nearest body surface and has no cloth-to-cloth contact, so it is not used.
- Trouser legs are straight and posed apart, and a tight front rise shows as stretch at the crotch point rather than as drag lines. The legacy skirt dialog shares the solver and fabric library but keeps its own body proxy (a surface of revolution); body and project-conversion unification remain unfinished. Cloth-to-cloth contact is limited to an outfit's top resting outside its bottoms, or tucked into them (a radial envelope round the body): tucked fabric is held in a thin layer rather than bunching, and in trousers the seat below the hip does not feel it, so a long top's tail can pass through the seat seam unseen; a hood lies over the back as a single layer, without contact with the body panels. The body is a proxy, not a personalized avatar: height, waist-to-hip depth and an underbust adjust its proportions, with remaining landmarks based on typical fractions (the bust points sit about 6 cm below the armpit, about 18 cm apart for a 100 cm bust, as two spheres rather than a measured shape); this is not a body scan.
- Every garment is meshed from its own pattern pieces, but those are simplified finished shapes, not graded production patterns: a top's front armhole scoops a fixed 1.2 cm deeper than the back near the underarm rather than being drafted from body measurements, the sleeve cap is a fixed symmetrical shape whose width is fitted to both armholes together, and hood and pocket shapes use fixed proportions. The hood's neck edge is drafted to the neckline's length; the hood starts folded back and drapes down the back. A polo's placket is drawn over the front in 3D with pictured buttons; the front pattern piece is not slit for it.
- Descriptions are read by a keyword parser with a fixed vocabulary. Small local language models were tried and not adopted (see the direction doc), and logos, graphics other than quoted text, and construction details are not read.
- Dresses are long tops: there is no waist seam, fitted bodice or woven dress block, and a dress hangs over a straight column below the hips (the legs together), so walking ease and slits are not modelled.
- Outfits are up to three garments (a top with bottoms, tucked in or not; a T-shirt under a sweatshirt or hoodie; or both with bottoms), with no jackets or coats, and a garment underneath is not simulated against the one over it (it is draped first and then pressed in where covered). No darts or pleats (gathers only at a skirt's waist, where the roughly 2 cm mesh shows them as soft flutes rather than fine gathers), no sketch-to-garment or reference-to-garment flow, and no rendering beyond the WebGL preview.
- Consumption, grading, construction steps and costing defaults are starting values. Confirm them with a marker, supplier quotes and a fit sample.
- An evaluation of the PPF Contact Solver did not lead to adopting it (see the direction doc). The app uses its own XPBD solver.

## Run locally

Prerequisites: Node.js 22.18+ (`engines`; CI uses 24) and npm. You need a current browser with WebGL, IndexedDB, Canvas and `createImageBitmap`. Without WebGL, pattern editing still works.

```sh
cd apps/clothing-studio
npm ci
npm run dev        # Vite dev server on 127.0.0.1; open the printed URL
```

Production build: `npm run build`, then `npm run preview`. The `dist/` output can be served by any static server. The production app makes no network requests.

## Verify

```sh
npm test               # Node unit tests (tests/*.test.ts)
npm run lint           # ESLint
npm run typecheck      # tsc --noEmit
npm run build          # typecheck + Vite production build
npm run check          # test + lint + build
npx playwright install chromium
npm run test:browser   # builds with the storage test harness, serves a preview on port 4186, runs tests/*.spec.ts
```

Use `CHROMIUM_PATH=/path/to/chromium` to run against an existing browser, and `CLOTHING_TEST_PORT` to change the port. CI: `.github/workflows/clothing-studio.yml` (`npm run check`, then the browser tests).

## Project layout

- `src/main.ts`, `src/workspace.ts`: app shell and the focused-workspace navigation.
- `src/product.ts`, `src/product-skirt.ts`, `src/product-view.ts`, `src/product-3d.ts`: base products, templates, fabrics and measurements; the product editor UI; the three.js garment.
- `src/garment-drape.ts`, `src/garment-drape.worker.ts`, `src/garment-comparison*.ts`: top draping and fabric comparison.
- `src/drape-test*.ts`: the fabric drape test (Cusick geometry) in the garment solver, its worker and its panel.
- `src/drape*.ts`, `src/construction*.ts`, `src/grading*.ts`, `src/print-pattern.ts`: skirt drape, construction, grading and tiled printing.
- `src/techpack.ts`, `src/range.ts`, `src/pattern-export.ts`: tech pack, range plan and costing, DXF/SVG export.
- `src/style-prompt.ts`, `src/design-prompt.ts`: description parsers for one part and for the whole garment.
- `src/tensile*.ts`, `src/bending*.ts`, `src/membrane*.ts`: swatch experiments (each with its worker).
- `src/model.ts`, `src/storage.ts`, `src/design-library*.ts`, `src/top-body-profiles*.ts`, `src/media.ts`: project model and validation, persistence, image admission.
- `tests/`: unit tests (`*.test.ts`) and Playwright specs (`*.spec.ts`).

## Data & privacy

- Everything stays in the browser. The current project autosaves to IndexedDB (`clothing-studio`), and saved named copies go to a second IndexedDB database (`clothing-studio-designs`). Saved body profiles are kept in `localStorage`. Unapplied drafts and Undo history last only for the session.
- Uploaded photos are resized to at most 1200 px and re-encoded as JPEG without metadata. Motifs and artwork are size-checked before decoding. Nothing is uploaded.
- Saved designs, backups and body-profile exports contain your photos, artwork and measurements. Clearing browser data removes the local library, so keep downloaded JSON backups of work you want to keep. Several tabs share one autosave, and the last write wins.
