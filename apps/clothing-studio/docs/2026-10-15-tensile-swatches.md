# Controlled tensile swatches — #266

This is the axial first milestone from the [material research](../../../docs/2026-10-14-clothing-material-physics-research.md), not a replacement garment solver. Body & drape contains an optional **Tensile swatch comparison**. Set coupon length/width, end force, 0° warp or90° weft and8/16 axial elements; compare two illustrative profiles, inspect a common-scale loaded/unloaded SVG and download the complete numerical JSON. Inputs/results are session-only and do not change the garment, body settings, current project, history or saved materials. Changing inputs invalidates export. Navigation retains the mounted experiment; cancellation, input changes and page departure retire the owned worker and reject its late reply.

## Model and units

Profiles are repository-authored demonstrations, not cotton/denim specimens or measured parameters. Compliant: density0.18kg/m², warp200N/m, weft100N/m. Stiff: density0.32kg/m², warp1200N/m, weft600N/m. Bending is explicitly unavailable; shear, transverse contraction, damping, thickness and contact are not modeled. No garment material is silently assigned one of these profiles.

For length L and width W in metres, directional extensional coefficient K in N/m, and n equal axial elements of length h=L/n, each element has stiffness k=KW/h (N/m). Minimize the dimensional potential `sum(k*(u[i+1]-u[i])²/2) - F*u[n]`, with u[0]=0 and a uniform total end force F in N. A tridiagonal linear solve gives the static equilibrium. Independent closed-form checks use extension `F*L/(K*W)`, mass `density*L*W`, stored energy `F*extension/2`, and coupon stiffness `K*W/L`. JSON distinguishes materialExtensionalNPerM from couponStiffnessNPerM; the two agree only when width equals length. End reaction and maximum internal/end force residual are recomputed from the element displacements.

Input lengths are named lengthCm/widthCm in cm; positions and extensions are m, mass kg, energy J, force N, strain dimensionless. Bounds:5–50cm lengths,0–5N force,0°/90° grain,8/16elements. Loads exceeding10% axial strain are refused before solving, not clamped. Profile coefficients must be positive finite values. Zero load remains undeformed. Density affects mass but does not alter this gravity-free static solution or elasticity.

This is a one-dimensional reduced coupon with no transverse displacement. It is not an orthotropic membrane, nonlinear cloth law, hanging swatch or drape simulation. Principal-direction material characterization motivates the separation of axes; see the primary [NASA orthotropic-material characterization report](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20170008800) and #256's cloth research. The axial energy/discretization here is our stated engineering model, not coefficients transferred from that report. No measured coefficients, reference observations or redistribution assumptions are introduced.

The solver has no time integration or triangles.8/16elements test equilibrium discretization, not mesh-diagonal independence or dynamic convergence. A future membrane/bending worker still needs the complete research gate: shear/coupling/material coordinates, geometry-normalized energy, timestep and mesh studies, separate calibration/validation observations, body/self/layer contact, seams, and physical garment comparison. The existing approximate XPBD top drape remains unchanged; physical fit and realistic material behavior are unvalidated.
